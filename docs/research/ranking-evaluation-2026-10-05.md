# Ranking evaluation for KINA (2026-10-05)

Question: how should KINA rank distributor candidates against a free-text request, and what role, if any, should the
Laya decision model play? This report evaluates the current ranker and 40+ alternatives on a new labelled set of 32
requests and 1259 real candidates. Every number below comes from a script in `scripts/research/` (see its `README.md`);
score files for every method are committed in `scripts/research/out/scores/`, and the tables are regenerated by
`evaluate.py`, `report_tables.py` and `select_cv.py`.

## 1. Summary

- **The current production blend makes ranking worse.** Deterministic ranker alone: NDCG@10 0.892. Production blend
  (deterministic + 0.2 x rank-normalised Laya `multilingual` noul): 0.823, a loss of 0.069 (95% CI -0.143 to -0.011).
  The cost is 2.4 s of CPU per query.
- **Laya zero-shot is weak in every shape tried.** Best of 20 variants (question shapes, state formats, token windows,
  all three checkpoints, embeddings, `predict_shortlist`, pairwise Bradley-Terry): 0.669 (`typed-decisions` noul).
  Every one of them is significantly below the deterministic ranker.
- **The deterministic ranker is strong and nearly free.** It is near perfect on passives (0.988) and good on discretes,
  ICs and connectors (0.90 to 0.93), but weak on vague requests (0.620). It runs in under 5 ms for 40 candidates.
  Learned weights on its features do not beat the hand-set ones (0.868 / 0.876 under leave-one-query-out).
- **A small cross-encoder adds a real, modest gain as a second signal.** `cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2`
  (22.7 M parameters, Apache-2.0) is 0.874 alone. A 50/50 rank-normalised blend with the deterministic score gives 0.913
  (+0.021, CI +0.003 to +0.041). Fine-tuned on synthetic rubric labels plus half the real labels, it gives 0.917 (+0.025,
  CI +0.007 to +0.045), and the gain holds after leave-one-query-out selection of the blend (0.917).
- **Laya fine-tuning is where its accuracy comes from, but it still trails.** A full CPU fine-tune of the multilingual
  checkpoint (encoder + head, 3 epochs, about 650 labelled decisions per fold, 8 to 9 minutes per fold on 16 E-cores)
  lifts Laya from 0.555 to 0.828 on held-out queries. As a 0.3 rank-blend partner it reaches 0.907. That is still
  below the 23 M-parameter cross-encoder blend, and it costs 2 s of CPU and a 2 GB sidecar per query.
- **Recommendation:** keep the deterministic ranker primary, take Laya out of the default ranking path, and add a
  MiniLM cross-encoder as a second signal in a 50/50 rank blend. Run it in-process through ONNX Runtime for Java behind
  the existing `PartRanker` interface, with the deterministic order as the fallback. Section 9 has the plan.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Dataset

`docs/research/data/ranking-eval.jsonl`, documented in `docs/research/data/README.md`.

- 32 requests: 10 passive, 6 discrete, 7 IC, 4 crystal/connector, 5 vague natural-language.
- 1259 candidates, 20 to 40 per query. Labels: 0 = 166, 1 = 342, 2 = 292, 3 = 459.
- Distributors: LCSC 1004, Mouser 170, TME 85.
- Sources:
  - 413 candidates from the running KINA stack (15 stack queries, 13 of them new).
  - 289 from KINA's own LCSC retrieval for the query text.
  - 557 hard negatives mined with KINA's LCSC retrieval for perturbed queries, from the full 7.1 M-part JLCPCB database.

Relevance scale, applied through a written rubric per query (stored in each record):

| label | meaning |
|---|---|
| 3 | meets every stated requirement (for vague requests: the interpretation written before scoring) |
| 2 | right type and primary value or function; one secondary requirement violated, or requirements not verifiable from the part data |
| 1 | same broad family but wrong primary value, two or more violations, or a near family (tantalum for MLCC, resistor array, other diode type, comparator for op amp) |
| 0 | different component type |

Labels were produced by rubric functions written independently of KINA's Java parsers (`scripts/research/queries.py`).
Every label was then reviewed by hand, and 10 corrections were recorded with reasons. The vague-query rubrics were
written before any method was scored.

### 2.2 Metrics

`scripts/research/evaluate.py` computes each metric per query, then takes the mean over the 32 queries.

| metric | definition |
|---|---|
| NDCG@5, NDCG@10 | gain 2^label - 1 |
| P@3 | share of the top 3 with label 3 |
| MRR | reciprocal rank of the first label-3 candidate |
| Spearman | rank correlation between the method's scores and the labels |

Ties in a method's scores are broken by a fixed hash of the candidate key, the same for every method.

For each method, the difference in NDCG@10 against the deterministic ranker has a paired bootstrap 95% confidence
interval (10 000 query resamples).

Many hybrids were tried on the same 32 queries, so the best one is optimistically biased. `select_cv.py` therefore also
reports a leave-one-query-out selection: the configuration is chosen on 31 queries and scored on the 32nd.

### 2.3 Hardware and latency protocol

- **Host:** Intel Core Ultra 9 285K, 8 P-cores (cpus 0-7) and 16 E-cores (8-23), 30 GB RAM.
- **Accuracy runs:** done in parallel. Laya on the P-cores; sentence-transformers and fine-tunes on E-cores, marked
  `_ecore`.
- **Latency runs:** latency numbers in the tables come from `latency.py`, re-run on P-cores with nothing else
  running: 8 threads on cpus 0-7 and 4 threads on cpus 0-3. A Laya measurement is the wall clock of one
  `/v1/systemone/batch` request holding every candidate of the query; a sentence-transformers or ONNX measurement is
  the scoring of every candidate from scratch.
- **RAM:** for Laya, the server container's memory after the run with one checkpoint loaded; for in-process models,
  the peak RSS of the scoring process.

### 2.4 Methods

| group | methods | implementation |
|---|---|---|
| a. baselines | `det` (production `DeterministicRanker`), distributor order, BM25 | `det` calls the real Java classes through `ResearchRunner` (a JUnit-free runner in package `ro.alacrity.kina.search`). It decomposes each score into a feature vector, and the runner aborts if the parts do not add back up to the production score. |
| b. Laya zero-shot over HTTP | noul (current), score scale, 4-option choice, 4 per-attribute nouls (mean / product), plain-text state, state with the parsed query attributes, `max_len` 192 / `head_max_len` 64, checkpoints `multilingual`, `english`, `typed-decisions` | `score_laya_http.py` |
| c. Laya pairwise | A / B / equal choice per pair; Bradley-Terry fit after 1, 2, 4, 8 rounds (n/2 pairs per round); round robin inside the deterministic top 10 | `score_laya_pairwise.py` |
| d. Laya SDK | encoder embeddings (`embed_fn_from_agent`, cosine to plain text or to the JSON state); `predict_shortlist` (k=10) with one choice over candidates | `score_laya_sdk.py` |
| e. other neural | bi-encoders `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, `bge-small-en-v1.5`; cross-encoders `ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2`, `ms-marco-MiniLM-L12-v2`, `bge-reranker-base`; ONNX Runtime fp32 / int8 of MiniLM-L6 | `score_neural.py`, `score_onnx.py` |
| f. learned weights | ridge on the label and pairwise logistic over the 10 deterministic features, leave-one-query-out | `score_learned.py` |
| g. hybrids | production formula `(1-w) det + w ranknorm(X)`; rank blend `(1-w) ranknorm(det) + w ranknorm(X)`, both for w in {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5}; X only breaks exact ties; X inside deterministic bands of 0.05; X re-orders the deterministic top 10; X on candidates with det >= 0.5 | `score_hybrids.py` |
| h. fine-tuning | Laya typed head only (2-fold); Laya head on synthetic labels; Laya full fine-tune (2-fold); MiniLM cross-encoder on real (2-fold), synthetic, and synthetic + real (2-fold) labels | `laya_finetune.py`, `ce_finetune.py`, `synth_data.py` |

**Which deterministic ranker.** `det` is not a re-implementation. It is the production Java code of this branch,
compiled from the worktree's `src/main`. That is `DeterministicRanker.java` / `QueryParser.java` as of commit `af48f11`
(branch base `00d6978`).

While this study ran, `main` gained connector-aware parsing and ranking (commit `bf1d59b`). That revision is also
scored, as `det_main_bf1d59b`, by `score_det_ref.py`: it compiles `git archive main src/main` and runs the same
`ResearchRunner`, which tolerates the changed feature decomposition. Result: 0.898 vs 0.892, all of it from
crystal/connector queries (0.955 vs 0.910). Every other category is unchanged.

The hybrids keep their gains on top of it: rank blend 0.5 with the fine-tuned cross-encoder gives 0.917, and the
production Laya blend falls to 0.858.

All other `det` rows in this report use `af48f11`.

The 2-fold split is by query and stratified by category. Each fold trains on one half and is evaluated on the other,
so every query is scored by a model that never saw it.

The synthetic data comes from `synth_data.py`: 80 random passive requests, 2872 candidates retrieved by KINA's LCSC
search, labelled by the same rubric functions. Value and package combinations used by the evaluation queries are
excluded.

Observations:

- **None of the variants is usable as a ranker.** Changing the question shape moves NDCG@10 by about 0.1. Changing the
  checkpoint moves it by about 0.1 as well, but `english` and `typed-decisions` cost 3x the CPU of `multilingual`.
- **Token windows are not the problem.** States average 98 to 124 tokens, well inside the window, so `max_len` and
  `head_max_len` changes do nothing.
- **Giving Laya the parsed attributes does not help.** The model does not use them.
- **Calibration and `min_confidence` cannot fix the ranking.** Temperature scaling is monotonic per question type, and
  the abstention gate only marks answers, so neither changes the order. `decide`/`decide_batch` project the same
  `predict_batch` outputs onto a schema, which adds nothing for ranking.

### 5.4 Fine-tuning feasibility (research question h)

The repository's recipe:

- `notebooks/laya_finetune_typed_decisions_2xT4_kaggle.ipynb` plus a single-process CPU/MPS port,
  `notebooks/laya_finetune_typed_decisions_mps.py`. The loss is RLCD: a policy-gradient term with annealed noise plus
  soft cross-entropy against teacher probabilities, followed by a temperature fit.
- The published typed-decisions model was trained on 6000 decisions. That took 4 to 6 minutes on 2x T4; about 30k
  questions take 4 to 5 hours.
- No GPU is required. We ran the same loss on CPU.

Measured on 16 E-cores, multilingual checkpoint (322 M parameters), one noul question per candidate, soft target
P(true) = label / 3:

| run | train items | train time | peak RAM | NDCG@10 on held-out queries |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| zero-shot (reference) | 0 | - | - | 0.555 |
| typed head only, encoder frozen, 8 epochs, 2-fold | 660 / 599 | 202 s / 177 s (+30 s encoder cache) | 4.5 GB | 0.651 |
| typed head only on 2872 synthetic labels, scored on all 32 | 2872 | 856 s | 7.4 GB | 0.605 |
| full fine-tune (encoder + head), first 10 steps only, fold A | 80 | 20 s (2.0 s per step of 8) | 8.0 GB | 0.697 (15 queries) |
| full fine-tune (encoder + head), 3 epochs, 2-fold | 660 / 599 | 565 s / 493 s | 8.3 GB | **0.828** (rank blend 0.3 with det: 0.907) |

Conclusions on fine-tuning:

- **A CPU fine-tune is feasible.** A full epoch over about 650 decisions takes about 3 minutes on 16 E-cores.
- **Data is the bottleneck, not compute.** About 650 hand-labelled decisions per fold move Laya from 0.555 to 0.828.
  That is the largest single improvement in this study, but it is still below the deterministic ranker (0.892).
  Freezing the encoder and training only the typed head is not enough (0.651).
- **Where the fine-tuned model is good and where it is not.** It is strong on discretes (0.972) and crystals/connectors
  (0.960), weak on passives (0.886) and vague requests (0.402). The typed-decisions result (0.36 to 0.77) needed 6000 teacher-labelled decisions in one
  narrow domain; for part ranking we would need a similar number per category family, mostly for the hard, non-parametric
  requests.
- **Synthetic rubric labels help the passives that do not need help.** They raise the head-only model on passives to
  0.769 but hurt it elsewhere (discrete 0.568). The same synthetic data is much more useful for the small cross-encoder
  (section 6).
- **The realistic way to get the labels is an LLM teacher.** Claude, which already calls KINA, could produce the soft
  targets the RLCD loss wants. That is a data project, not a model choice, and it would benefit the cross-encoder just
  as much.

- **Cross-encoders beat bi-encoders here.** The cross-encoder reads the query and the part together, so it can check

- **The smallest cross-encoder is as good as the 12x larger `bge-reranker-base`** in the blend (0.913 for both), so

  found in this study.

How to read the hybrids:

- **The production formula gains little even from a good model, and loses badly with a noisy one.** It adds a
  rank-normalised signal to a raw deterministic score whose good candidates sit in a narrow band (about 0.7 to 0.95),
  so even w = 0.2 of a noisy signal reshuffles the top. With both sides rank-normalised, the cross-encoder adds
  information without that distortion.
- **Laya hurts in every hybrid except the tie-breaker,** and as a tie-breaker it is neutral (-0.003) at a cost of 2.4 s
  per query.
- **Selection-corrected estimates (`select_cv.py`, configuration chosen on 31 queries, scored on the 32nd):**

  | family | configurations | LOQO NDCG@10 | chosen |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | det + MiniLM-L6 CE | 12 | 0.913 | rank blend 0.5 every time |
  | det + fine-tuned CE | 12 | 0.917 | rank blend 0.5 every time |
  | det + Laya ml noul | 12 | 0.884 | tie-breaker |
  | det + any of the 14 signals | 168 | 0.903 | the fine-tuned CE rank blend 31 times out of 32 |

  So the CE blend's gain is not a selection artefact.

**Native candidates only.** Restricting each set to what distributors actually returned (no mined negatives; 27 queries
with at least 5 candidates and a label-3 part) makes every set easier. Results: det 0.946, MiniLM CE rank blend 0.950,
fine-tuned CE rank blend 0.952, BM25 0.939, production Laya blend 0.903, distributor order 0.884. The ordering of
methods is the same; the margins shrink because the hard negatives are what separates rankers.

## 9. Recommendation

KINA should keep the deterministic ranker as the primary signal. It should remove Laya from the default ranking path,
because on this evidence every Laya configuration that changes the order makes it worse, and the one harmless role
(tie-breaker) costs 2.4 s of CPU per search for nothing. It should add a MiniLM cross-encoder
(`ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2`, preferably fine-tuned on rubric-generated synthetic labels) as a second signal, combined as
`0.5 x ranknorm(det) + 0.5 x ranknorm(CE)`. That gave +0.021 to +0.025 NDCG@10 overall and the largest gains on
discretes, ICs and connectors, without hurting passives or vague requests. The model is 23 M parameters and about
91 MB as ONNX; it needs about 165 ms (median, 335 ms worst) for 40 candidates on 8 cores, so it fits the 20 s budget fifty times
over. It can run inside the KINA JVM with ONNX Runtime, which removes the sidecar container and the semaphore.

Laya should stay only as an optional, off-by-default `PartRanker` implementation. It can be re-evaluated with
`evaluate.py` if someone trains a part-ranking checkpoint on thousands of teacher-labelled decisions, because
fine-tuning is the only route that moved its numbers.

### 9.1 Implementation plan (against the existing `PartRanker` interface)

1. **Fix the blend first (no new model).** Change `RankingService` to rank-normalise the deterministic scores before
   blending, `final = (1 - w) * ranknorm(det) + w * ranknorm(model)`, and set `kina.ranking.laya.enabled=false` by
   default.
   - Rationale: section 7 shows the current formula with the current Laya signal is the main regression (0.823 vs
     0.892).
   - Keep the tie-break chain (deterministic, stock, price).
2. **New `CrossEncoderPartRanker implements PartRanker` (`name() = "cross-encoder"`).**
   - Inputs: the request text and, per candidate, the same plain rendering as `candidate_text` in
     `scripts/research/common.py`: manufacturer, MPN, description, category, package, attributes. Enriched comparable
     attributes are included.
   - Output: raw logits; `RankingService` rank-normalises them.
   - Dependencies: `com.microsoft.onnxruntime:onnxruntime` (CPU, about 10 MB native) and a WordPiece tokenizer. Use
     `ai.djl.huggingface:tokenizers` (Apache-2.0, reads `tokenizer.json`) rather than hand-writing BERT tokenisation.
   - Session: one `OrtSession` per JVM, `intraOpNumThreads` from `kina.ranking.cross-encoder.threads` (default 4),
     batch of up to 64 pairs, `max_length` 256.
   - Model files: `model.onnx` plus `tokenizer.json` on a volume. They can be downloaded on first start like the
     JLCPCB database, or baked into the image (about 90 MB fp32; int8 is smaller but see section 8).
   - Budget: keep `kina.ranking.timeout` and the deterministic fallback with `RankingMode.FALLBACK`. Keep the score
     cache keyed by query and part.
3. **Configuration.**
   - `kina.ranking.ranker = deterministic | cross-encoder | laya`, default `cross-encoder` once step 4 is done, and
     `deterministic` until then.
   - `kina.ranking.blend-weight = 0.5`, `kina.ranking.max-candidates = 40` (unchanged).
   - Keep `LayaPartRanker` selectable; drop `laya-serve` from the default compose file, or move it to an overlay like
     `compose.cuda.yaml`.
4. **Model artefact pipeline.**
   - Export `cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2` with optimum (`score_onnx.py` shows the exact call).
   - Then fine-tune it on synthetic rubric data plus the labelled set (`ce_finetune.py synth_real`, extended to all 32
     queries for the shipped model), export again, and check that `evaluate.py` reproduces at least the 2-fold numbers.
   - Version the model file name and record its NDCG@10 in the image labels.
5. **Tests.**
   - A unit test with a stub session.
   - A golden test that runs the real ONNX model on the `LayaRankerEvaluationTest` fixtures (10 candidates) and on 3
     queries from `ranking-eval.jsonl`, asserting the top 3.
   - Keep `ranking-eval.jsonl` as a regression set: a CI job (or a manual `-Dtest`) that fails if NDCG@10 drops more
     than 0.01.
6. **Improve the deterministic ranker where the cross-encoder showed it is blind** (separate, cheap changes):
   - interface type words: RS-485 vs CAN vs LIN
   - connector pin count and row count (`1x4P`)
   - TVS polarity
   - Zener nominal voltage when the description also lists a min~max range
   - vague requests ("decoupling" means 100 nF MLCC; "pull-up" means 2.2k to 10k), possibly through a small synonym
     table in `QueryParser`

   Re-run `evaluate.py` after each change.

## 10. What could not be evaluated, and caveats

- **Sample size.** 32 queries: effects below about 0.02 NDCG@10 are not resolvable (see the CIs). LCSC makes up 80 % of
  candidates because of the Mouser call cap.
- **Mouser calls.** 14 distinct queries were sent to the stack, and the decoupling query triggered KINA's phrase
  fallback, so 15 Mouser API calls in total. Two further stack queries were already cached and free.
- **GPU latency** was not measured (no GPU on this host). The project's T4 figures (33 ms for one question, 7 ms per
  additional one) suggest Laya would fit easily on a GPU, but accuracy, not speed, is its problem.
- **Laya `english` and `typed-decisions` checkpoints** were evaluated only with the noul and choice questions (the two
  best shapes on `multilingual`). Pairwise used `multilingual` only, because its per-pair judgements were near noise
  and the other checkpoints cost 3x per state.
- **Fine-tuned cross-encoder for production.** The 2-fold numbers are what to expect on new queries; a model trained on
  all 32 queries was not evaluated, because no held-out data would remain.
- **Version difference.** One cross-encoder fine-tune (`synth`) was started by mistake on a transformers 4.57 build of
  the research image. It was killed and re-run on the transformers 5.18 image used for everything else.
- **Label bias.** The labelling rubric is parametric by nature, which favours rule-based rankers. The cross-encoder's
  wins on non-parametric queries happened despite this.
